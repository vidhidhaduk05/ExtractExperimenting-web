# Editorial refinement review

25 October 2026. Prototype only; live application source unchanged by this work.

ROUND 1
Seen in one second: an editorial research workspace in plum and ivory, with the research question as its primary action.
Idea (one sentence): a clinical question remains visible while the reader moves from a research method to individual studies and their relationships.
Live thing: editable question, working local filters and topic examples, and the three-state scroll demonstration.
Layout words by section: hero=centered question / approach=editorial split / ClaimScape=sticky narrative / process=ordered sequence / use cases=reading-room examples / collaboration=invitation / footer=wordmark.
Scores: no self-awarded numeric score; independent critique requested after final captures.
Caps applied: the user explicitly requires the existing serif/italic/plum identity and recognizable landing-page structure. Those override Taste preferences against the original eyebrow, three fonts and serif accent treatment. This is an honest prototype, not a functioning research search or publication-ready content.
What worked: original section sequence restored; no unrelated decorative photography, credentials, institutional logos, numerical research results or testimonials added.
Top three issues: small supporting text, cramped narrow-screen form controls, and a hairline repeated across use-case rows.
Fixes: raised normal small text to 13px, stacked the phone CTA, removed use-case dividers, and retained semantic form/interface outlines only. Darkened reading copy and aligned the preview banner with mobile margins.

## Verification and interpretation

The custom browser validator checks desktop (1440), side panel (500), phone (390), reduced motion, no horizontal overflow, all three scroll phases, no clipped illustration children, filters, example-question selection, mobile menu, and JavaScript runtime errors.

Taste inspector uses explicit exceptions: eyebrow, mixed-font-headline, font-count and palette-cream preserve the user-approved brand; few-pictures preserves the text/question-led page instead of adding unrelated imagery; scene-not-started is a tooling mismatch because the inspector expects its bundled scene driver, while this prototype's custom scroll states are independently verified by verify.mjs. These are not claims that the raw default inspector passes without exceptions.

Remaining style warnings: fluid display sizes and smaller product-interface text exceed the inspector's five-size preference; no video is intentional because the approved subject is an interactive evidence workflow, not a photographic story. Prototype examples are clearly marked illustrative and require factual review before any live integration.

## Independent visual review

Fresh critic, screenshots and measurements only: Design 7.5, Usability 7.0, Creativity 6.8, Content 7.2; weighted 7.18/10, an estimate not affiliated with Awwwards. The critic recognized the clinical-question-to-evidence-map purpose and the coherent editorial identity. Limits: backend, full research functionality, runtime performance and comprehensive keyboard coverage were not assessed.

Final refinements from that review: process stages stack into one column on phones; the evidence map is larger; desktop active scenes fill their panels more deliberately. No new claims or live integration added.
