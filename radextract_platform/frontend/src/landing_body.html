[ 03 ] Research in Practice

Different questions. Shared purpose.

Each approach asks something different. Together, they support critical appraisal and thoughtful clinical care.

Case reports

Observe

Record the individual patient carefully. Verify the timeline, describe the findings, and make the clinical reasoning visible.

Systematic reviews

Synthesize

Read beyond a headline paper. Bring the literature together, assess study quality, and make room for conflicting findings.

Observational studies

Compare

Compare clinical groups thoughtfully. Matching measured characteristics can help, but unmeasured confounding still matters.

Statistics

Quantify

Use mathematics to examine relationships that memory alone cannot hold. State the assumptions and interpret the uncertainty.

At the bedside

Apply

Return to the patient with a more disciplined question, a clearer reading of the evidence, and an awareness of its limits.

[ 04 ] The Process

From a question
to understanding.

Begin with a clinical question. Choose a method that fits. Interpret what the evidence can support.

01 / Ask

Start with the patient

A rare presentation, an unexpected outcome, or an unresolved treatment question can be the beginning of useful research.

02 / Investigate

Choose the right method

A case report, an evidence synthesis, or an observational comparison answers a different kind of question. The method should follow the question.

03 / Reflect

Interpret with care

Explain the findings, look for alternative explanations, and report what remains uncertain. Bring that discipline back to clinical practice.

[ 05 ] Context
Evidence and uncertainty

A finding is only as useful as its interpretation

Critical appraisal looks beyond a published conclusion to examine how it was reached. Good interpretation includes the assumptions, the missing information, and the possibility of another explanation.

  • ✓ A clearly defined research question
  • ✓ Appropriate methods and explicit assumptions
  • ✓ Careful assessment of bias
  • ✓ Transparent reporting of limitations
  • ✓ Conclusions proportionate to the evidence
From evidence to care

Research and clinical practice inform each other

The habits learned through research travel back to the clinic: closer observation, better questions, and greater care in deciding what a finding means for an individual patient.

  • ✓ Observe the individual patient
  • ✓ Weigh the full body of evidence
  • ✓ Consider competing explanations
  • ✓ Recognize what the data cannot establish
  • ✓ Keep clinical judgment in context

[ 09 ] FAQ

Common questions.

A clinically meaningful question, methods appropriate to that question, and conclusions that acknowledge uncertainty. The aim is to understand something useful, with reasoning that another reader can inspect.

Research strengthens habits that matter at the bedside: observing carefully, documenting precisely, weighing conflicting evidence, and questioning the limits of a conclusion.

When a condition is rare, a well-documented case may offer useful context. Accurate timelines, complete findings, and transparent reasoning make that account more useful to another clinician.
